# Trust model

What you have to trust when you use Strike, what you can check yourself instead, and what happens if each part misbehaves. "Trusted" means a party could act against you and nothing on-chain would stop it; "verified" means you can check its output yourself, from the chain, without trusting us. Every check below is read-only and needs no key.

The short version: the contracts are immutable and enforce each vault's mandate; the admin powers they keep are listed below and visible on-chain; on mainnet the price comes straight from Chainlink. On the testnets the price comes from a `MirrorFeed` that our keeper fills. Until 2 October that was the last input you had to take on our word. Now every value on it is checked against the mainnet Chainlink round it copies ([D40](decisions.md#d40--a-price-mirror-audit-makes-the-testnet-prices-checkable-2026-10-02)). What remains trusted there is when the keeper pushes, not what it pushes.

## What the price mirror audit checks, exactly

For each `MirrorFeed` round on a testnet (all rounds, or `--symbol`, `--rounds N`, `--since <time>`):

1. Read the round's `answer` and `updatedAt` (`getRoundData`), and its push transaction from the feed's `AnswerUpdated` log.
2. Find the mainnet Chainlink round with the same `updatedAt` on the proxy the keeper copies (`MAINNET_FEED` in `scripts/keeper.sh`, `MAINNET_CHAINLINK_FEEDS` in the SDK). Proxy round ids are `phase << 64 | aggregator round`; the search walks every phase the window covers, batched through Multicall3.
3. Classify the round: **match** (same timestamp, same answer), **answer differs** (a mainnet round at that time printed something else), **no mainnet round** (mainnet printed nothing at that time: a fabricated or re-timed price), **future timestamp** (later than mainnet's head). Any of the last three fails the audit and the command exits 1.
4. Check that each deployment's `EpochManager` reads its recorded `StockOracle` (`oracle()`) and that each `StockOracle` reads the audited feed for every listed token (`feedConfig(token).feed`), so the checked prices are the ones sales and settlement use. A swap by the admin (`setOracle`, `setFeed`) fails the audit; it is detected, not prevented.

It also reports what it cannot judge: the largest gap between pushes, how many mainnet prints fell in each feed's window and how many the keeper mirrored, and for a settlement whether the round used is mainnet's first print at or after expiry (the round a mainnet settlement would use). The code is [`sdk/src/mirror.ts`](../sdk/src/mirror.ts) (`auditMirror`, `auditSettlement`), with tests for a tampered answer, a fabricated timestamp, a missing mainnet round, a future timestamp, a phase change, the deploy seed, a swapped feed, a swapped oracle and the settlement cases in [`sdk/test/mirror.test.ts`](../sdk/test/mirror.test.ts).

## What is still on trust, in one list

- **When the keeper pushes.** It pushes the latest mainnet round when it runs, and it does not run continuously: on 46630 it mirrored 15 of the 41 TSLA mainnet prints between its first and its last push. A dishonest keeper could time its pushes. The settlement check shows whether the settlement round is mainnet's first print after expiry.
- **The admin roles**, until mainnet, where they sit with a Safe. Every use is an event, but a compromised admin can choose a settlement price before it is recorded (`setFeed`, `setOracle`); the mirror audit detects that on the testnets, it does not prevent it. A recorded price is final.
- **Chainlink**, as the data source, on mainnet and (through the mirror) on the testnets.
- **The token issuers.**
- **Your RPC**, for every read, including the audit's.
